# linux-omarchy-aarch64

The `linux-omarchy` kernel for aarch64, with NVIDIA N1x (RTX Spark) and Snapdragon laptop support added. This folder builds the packages `linux-omarchy` and `linux-omarchy-headers` for aarch64, as the `linux-omarchy` folder does for x86_64; until 7.2.8-8 they were named `linux-omarchy-n1x` and `linux-omarchy-n1x-headers`, which the new ones replace on upgrade. The folder's name is the recipe's `pkgbase`, which is how the build and publish tooling keeps the two recipes' files apart.

It carries the signed Omarchy patch set of `linux-omarchy` 7.2.8-10, ported to 7.3, plus N1x topic patches numbered 1000 and up. `linux-omarchy` is the BORE build (it was `linux-omarchy-bore` until 7.2.8-9, which is now a transitional package), so the shared patches, their signatures and the BORE and ADIOS schedulers come from `linux-omarchy`, byte for byte, including its BORE rebases of the scheduler patches. This package moved to 7.3 ahead of `linux-omarchy`, which is still on 7.2.8. Twelve of the shared patches 7.3 still needs had to change for it and carry no signature. `0110` is BORE's own 7.3 patch, `0450` the amd-staging HDMI VRR and ALLM series, `0608` only the icm fix 7.3 lacks, and `0140`, `0350`, `0402`, `0460`, `0461`, `0478`, `0517`, `0804` and `8205` are rebuilt for 7.3. The rest are `linux-omarchy`'s files with their signatures. `0568` and `0571` stay out: 7.3 has the HID updates, and `0571` reverts the Logitech Bolt receiver support `0568` adds (upstream 3fabd8ec206b, in 7.3-rc6), which made mice on a Bolt receiver scroll about 15 times too fast. Once `linux-omarchy` moves to 7.3, this package takes its set again. The intent is to fold these into `linux-omarchy` itself once it builds for aarch64; until then this is the kernel Omarchy installs on N1x machines (`install/hardware/n1x.sh` in omarchy), replacing NVIDIA's 7.0-based `linux-n1x`.

The `pkgrel` counts N1x revisions of this package and starts again at 1 when the kernel version changes. On 7.2.5 it was `6.1` to `6.9` while it tracked `linux-omarchy`'s release; the ninth revision became `9`, `10` added the Dell XPS 16 patches, `11` brought a dock's displays back after a failed DisplayPort tunnel, `12` stopped the clock during suspend-to-idle, `13` brought NVIDIA's device sleep patches from 1022.23, `14` let keyboard and touchpad interrupts wake the system from the platform's deepest sleep, and `15` brought audio and UCSI fixes from upstream and turned kexec handover off by default. `7.2.8-1` moves to 7.2.8 and `linux-omarchy` 7.2.8-5's patch set, `2` closes a SoundWire interrupt race that could hang the boot, `3` stops `cppc_cpufreq` registering its artificial energy model, so EAS no longer packs the desktop onto the A725 cores, `4` ships the Snapdragon laptops' device trees and resets the Iris AHB bridge before hardware mode, `5` builds SCMI CPU frequency scaling in, which the Snapdragon laptops need, `6` lets suspend-to-idle reach the platform's deepest idle state, which brings sleep from 6-15 W to under 1 W, `7` stops reclaiming the GPU's reserved memory, which the firmware now sizes itself, `8` takes the name `linux-omarchy`, `9` registers `cppc_cpufreq`'s artificial energy model again, so that power-profiles-daemon's RTX Spark driver can turn EAS on and off with the governor, as NVIDIA designed it, `10` keeps a dock's tunnels through suspend-to-idle, so its USB devices and displays come back on resume, `11` spaces out a dock's DisplayPort teardowns, so the GPU sees both monitors leave when a two-monitor dock is unplugged, `13` keeps USB and the SSPM-dependent ACPI drivers working on a DGX Spark whose firmware hides the SSPM mailbox, `14` lets Bolt authorize new docks on its own, as it does on Intel and AMD laptops, `15` brings back the Snapdragon laptops' digital microphones, `16` lets UPower see a Snapdragon laptop's charger being plugged in and out, `17` adds the Surface Pro 11 patches, `19` lets the Snapdragon X1 laptops sleep with DDR in self-refresh and the SoC's CX rail collapsed, `20` turns on the Lenovo Yoga Slim 7x's webcam, `21` stops the camera bus controller from stalling when a camera starts again soon after it stopped, `22` stops the NVIDIA DGX Spark resetting when idle, and `23` keeps the Snapdragon laptops' Wi-Fi from stalling after the access point changes its channel or bandwidth and fixes an out-of-bounds read and a deadlock in the Iris video decoder. `7.3-1` moves to 7.3, ahead of `linux-omarchy`, `7.3-7` adds the ASUS Zenbook A16, `7.3-8` lets its Copilot key send F23, and `7.3-9` gives the ASUS ProArt P14 its keyboard back on BIOS 303.

[`UPGRADING-7.3.md`](UPGRADING-7.3.md) has notes for the move to 7.3: what each N1x patch needs there, what upstream already has, and how to verify the result.

## DGX Spark

`1099` leaves the firmware DVFSRC flag unchanged on the NVIDIA DGX Spark.
The N1x probe-time SYSRAM write triggers idle resets on that machine even
though it reports the same PLID. The exception matches the exact DMI vendor
and product; BestPerf and the rest of power_wrap remain enabled. Other GB10
products have not been tested, and the firmware reset mechanism is still
unknown.

## N1x patches

Patches 1000–1040 are NVIDIA's SAUCE from `Ubuntu-nvidia-7.0-7.0.0-1021.21_24.04.1` (commit `dd99802c8b0c384169bb36293c9517fa67238e47`), rebased onto 7.3 with Omarchy's patches applied and grouped by topic:

- `1000-n1x-mediatek-platform.patch` - MediaTek MT8901 I/O: pinctrl, GPIO, I2C (keyboard, touchpad and touch panel are HID-over-I2C behind it), xHCI, SSPM and power_wrap
- `1010-n1x-ffa-ec.patch` - the ARML0002 FF-A embedded controller (battery, AC, lid, thermal zones, UCSI)
- `1020-n1x-power.patch` - CPPC autonomous mode, GIC, watchdog, pmdomain and idle fixes
- `1030-n1x-audio.patch` - MT8901 SoundWire and ASoC (CS42L43 + CS35L56 on the ASUS ProArt P14), plus a fix for 7.2's `asoc_sdw_parse_sdw_endpoints()` signature
- `1040-n1x-gpu-iommu.patch` - DMA-mode SMMU domains for the integrated GPU (10de:2e00–2e3f); without them GSP fails to boot and the screen stays black

Patches 1011–1013 are our own, for the Dell XPS 16 (DX16263):

- `1011` fills the battery status from EC RAM. Dell's firmware answers the EC battery service's GetBst with zeros, so the battery read empty at 0 V.
- `1012` skips the PSYS/PSOC thermal zones on the XPS 16. There they report power, not temperature, and showed up as zones at 600–1245 °C. It is limited to that model until the ProArt P14's zones are checked.
- `1013` adds `dell-arm64-hotkeys`, which reports the hotkeys Dell's firmware sends as WMI events (mic mute) without the x86-only ACPI-WMI core. The XPS 16's EC never sends its FF-A notifications on BIOS 1.2.0, so on that machine the driver polls the EC's event queue instead (every 250 ms). Its mic-mute LED can only be toggled and stays lit across reboots, so the driver tracks it as `platform::micmute` (which Omarchy's mute script sets) and turns it off at shutdown.

`1021-ACPI-processor_idle-let-LPI-states-freeze-the-tick-in-suspend-to-idle.patch` is our own. Without it, suspend-to-idle on ACPI LPI idle states left timekeeping running, so CLOCK_MONOTONIC kept advancing while the laptop slept. Any sleep longer than three minutes made systemd kill systemd-logind, systemd-journald and boltd for missed watchdog pings as soon as it woke. That dropped the graphical session to the login screen and left NetworkManager asleep with no network. The CPUs enter the same idle state as before; the tick and timekeeping are now frozen as well. It sits on Rafael Wysocki's `_LPI` parsing rework, which is in 7.3 (on 7.2.8 Omarchy's `0210-pm-updates.patch` brought it).

Until 7.2.8-9 our `1022` stopped `cppc_cpufreq` registering its artificial energy model on the Cortex-X925 and Cortex-A725 cores, which kept EAS off. The firmware gives the two core types different MADT efficiency classes, and the model charges each higher class the energy of four CPUs, so the X925 cores look over ten times as expensive and EAS keeps whatever fits on the A725 cores. That went unnoticed while schedutil chose their frequency. Once `omarchy-power-profiles-daemon`'s first driver ran balanced in CPPC autonomous selection at EPP 64, the firmware held those cores at their 338 MHz floor through Hyprland's short bursts, Hyprland stalled for 20 to 39 ms at a time, and the desktop lagged. NVIDIA's RTX Spark driver, which replaced it, uses the governor as the EAS switch: schedutil, which lets EAS run, in power-saver and in balanced on battery, and performance, which keeps it off, otherwise. Under that driver and Omarchy's rule restoring the CPPC range, EAS in balanced on battery kept Hyprland on the A725 cores with p99 IPC round trips of 1.7–2.3 ms and no stall over 8 ms; idle draw matched EAS off, and a bursty background load drew 17% less but ran 2.9 times slower. So the model is registered again, for that switch to work.

`1023-ACPI-processor_idle-let-suspend-to-idle-reach-the-NVIDIA-N1x-DRIPS-state.patch` and `1024-PCI-quirks-let-the-NVIDIA-N1x-NVMe-link-reach-L1.2-so-the-SoC-can-sleep.patch` are our own, and together they make suspend-to-idle sleep. The SoC's deepest idle state, Standby-DRIPS, is the system-level `_LPI` state, but until `\_SB.PEPD.ELST(1)` sets MPEL the firmware reports it with a minimum residency of 0xFFFFFFFF and an exit latency of 966 us, one below the package state's 967 us. Suspend-to-idle picks the deepest state by strictly increasing exit latency, so it never chose DRIPS. `1023` adds DRIPS's wake latency (0x19D2 us) to the exit latency of those states and leaves their residency alone, so suspend-to-idle reaches DRIPS while the idle governors still never pick it. Calling `ELST(1)` at boot, as Windows' platform extension does, is not an alternative: it gives DRIPS a 1.4 ms residency, the governors then enter it with devices running, and the boot hangs before Plymouth. In DRIPS, the SoC's power manager was then woken by PCIe about every 60 ms and never got past its shallowest platform level (PPS0, MCUSYS off). The firmware leaves ASPM off on every link, and the NVMe drive, though in D3hot, kept its link in plain L1 with CLKREQ# asserted. `1024` allows ASPM L1 and PCI-PM L1.1/L1.2 on the NVMe link through `pci_enable_link_state()`, on systems with the N1x platform extension (NVDA6210). The firmware's own telemetry (the SRAM page at 0x130000 that NVIDIA's Windows PEP reads) then shows a single DRIPS for the whole sleep at PPS4 (DRAM in self-refresh), woken only by the lid.

`1050-efi-add-efi_reclaim_reserved-to-use-idle-reserved-memory-as-RAM.patch` is our own: the N1x firmware reserves tens of GiB as the GPU's dedicated memory under Windows (62.5 GiB on the 128 GB ProArt P14), which Linux's driver never uses. `efi_reclaim_reserved=<size>@<start>,...` hands named ranges of it to the kernel, and is ignored unless each range is still entirely reserved, writeback-capable memory. Omarchy sets it per model from `install/hardware`.

`1060-ASoC-mediatek-mt8901-give-the-card-the-ACPI-subsystem-ID.patch` is also our own. Cirrus CS35L56 amplifiers name their DSP firmware and speaker tuning after the sound card's PCI subsystem ID, which the ACPI-enumerated MT8901 card does not have, so they ran on ROM defaults: quiet and unvoiced. The patch reads the SoundWire controller's `_SUB` (written device ID first, `33A11043` on the ProArt P14) and passes it to the card, so the amplifiers request `cs35l56-b0-dsp1-misc-104333a1-spkid0*`. Those files are not in linux-firmware yet.

Patches 1031 and 1032 are audio fixes from upstream that 7.3 does not have:

- `1031` (a41da0bb086e, in linux-next; NVIDIA carries it as e2f418271a2c, LP 2167235) gives `snd_hda_acpi`, the driver for the GPU's HDMI and DisplayPort audio controller (NVDA2014), runtime PM callbacks and calls them on system sleep. The driver never enabled runtime PM, so suspend left the controller running and resume never re-initialised it. After some resumes its codec commands timed out (`azx_get_response timeout, switching to polling mode`) and HDMI and DisplayPort audio stayed degraded or silent until reboot; on NVIDIA's board resume took 81 seconds. The controller is now stopped and its link reset on suspend, and re-initialised on resume. `1030` already carried NVIDIA's `pm_ptr()` follow-up to this commit, which until now only had the system sleep callbacks to wrap.
- `1032` is Cirrus' "ASoC: cs35l56: Wait for firmware timer expiry before system suspend", taken from the list (Mark Brown's bot reported it applied to `broonie/sound` for-7.3, but it is not in 7.3). It applies as posted. CS35L56 firmware older than 3.13.7 does not allow a SoundWire bus reset within 250 ms of starting a firmware timer, and system suspend could reset the bus inside that window. The ProArt P14's amplifiers run 3.13.4. The first amplifier to suspend now waits for the timer to expire, once for all of them.

`1034-soundwire-mediatek-close-the-interrupt-gate-before-quiescing-a-link.patch` is our own, a fix to NVIDIA's MediaTek SoundWire driver. Quiescing a link cleared `interrupt_enabled` and waited for the running status work before closing the controller's interrupt gate, and that work re-enables the slave interrupts as it finishes. A slave interrupt in that window was cleared but neither masked nor handled, and a peripheral at Dev0 raises it again at once. The link's attach check, 2 s after it comes up, hit the window: `7.2.8-1` escaped after a few hundred interrupts, but a test build with different boot timing hit it on every boot. The status work was queued on CPU0, which was handling the storm, so it never finished and the gate stayed open, about one `slave attached int` every 100 µs; the boot never got past Plymouth, and the fallback entry was still storming nine minutes in. The gate now closes first when a link is quiesced, the handler masks slave interrupts whenever it sees one, even when nothing will handle it, and its slave status messages are rate-limited.

The rt712-sdca PLL2 fix (d56fe35c1bce, NVIDIA's a241cbc5dd92) was `1033` on 7.2.5. It is in 7.3, so the package no longer carries it.

Patches 1070–1082 and 1085–1089 make USB4 and Thunderbolt work. The N1x host routers (`\_SB.UBF0..2`, `NVDA8100`, one per USB-C port) are ACPI platform devices rather than PCI NHIs, the firmware hands USB4 to the OS, and it has no connection manager of its own. Without these patches nothing bound them, so PCIe-tunnelled devices (docks, 10G adapters, eGPUs) never appeared, and USB4 docks fell back to USB-C alt modes.

- `1070` reverts NVIDIA's SAUCE that disabled USB4 through a vendor `_DSM`. The ASUS EC doesn't implement that `_DSM`, so the revert also removes a 2 s stall.
- `1071` has `ucsi_acpi` query the `_DSM` functions before using them, as the ACPI spec asks. The ProArt P14 EC opens its UCSI service on that query, and without it every boot logged `PPM init failed` and `/sys/class/typec` stayed empty.
- `1072` adds `power_wrap_drv.usb4_release=`. NVIDIA's power_wrap releases the host routers' SSPM resources once xHCI is up. After that the SSPM stops answering for them, and a later power request hangs it and resets the SoC. `1076` reports whether the release ran, so the driver can refuse to probe.
- `1073`–`1075` let the Thunderbolt core run on a host interface that is not a PCI device, building on 7.2's non-PCI NHI groundwork. On 7.2.8 the PCI glue keeps Omarchy's shutdown host reset (`nhi_pci_do_remove()`) and hands the rest of the teardown to `nhi_remove()`.
- `1077` is the glue driver itself, `thunderbolt_platform` (`CONFIG_USB4_PLATFORM_NHI`). It maps the host interface, powers it through power_wrap, and services the rings from the one wired level interrupt.
- `1078` fixes PCI bus numbering below a hot-added switch. Without it, `pci=hpbussize`, which the unconfigured tunnel root ports need, let a dock's first downstream port take every bus number, so the ports after it (on a CalDigit TS4, the Ethernet controller) were never enumerated.
- `1079` turns off CL states on the N1x host router's links. With them on, a monitor behind a DisplayPort tunnel failed to sync to its first link training and kept dropping out, so a dock's display usually stayed dark at boot and often on hotplug.
- `1080` keeps the routers out of USB4 sleep during suspend. A router only leaves that sleep through the reset it gets when its host interface loses power, which never happens here, so a port with a device attached stopped answering after resume.
- `1081` stops the driver powering the routers down when it is unbound. The SSPM can turn a router off, but turning it back on does not restore what the boot firmware set up, so the router stayed dead until the next boot.
- `1082` offers the N1x host router's DP IN adapters again when a monitor is plugged in. The connection manager drops a DP IN adapter whose tunnel fails DPRX negotiation until the adapter reports a hotplug, which the N1x's never do, so after one failure every display behind a dock on that port stayed black until reboot.
- `1085` keeps the tunnels of routers that `1080` keeps awake through suspend-to-idle. Resume treated those routers as reset: it tore down the tunnels it found and built them again, the USB3 tunnel did not get its bandwidth back, and the dock's xHCI stopped responding. The DP tunnels, torn down at suspend, waited for plug events that routers kept awake never send, so the dock's displays stayed dark until it was replugged.
- `1086` waits 100 ms between the DP tunnels it tears down when a dock goes away (the `dp_teardown_delay` module parameter). Each teardown drops HPD towards the GPU, and the N1x GPU firmware handled only the first of two drops a few milliseconds apart: it kept the other monitor connected, and that monitor stayed black after every replug until the next suspend.
- `1087`–`1089` let Bolt authorize new docks on its own, as it does on Intel and AMD laptops. Bolt only does that when the Thunderbolt domain reports IOMMU DMA protection (`iommu_dma_protection`), and the N1x reported none, so a new dock's display tunnel came up while its USB controller and Ethernet waited for a manual `boltctl enroll`. The firmware already marks the tunnel root ports with `ExternalFacingPort` and `usb4-host-interface`, but `1087` was needed for Linux to act on that outside x86: the check that marks tunnelled devices untrusted and removable read only those ACPI properties, yet was compiled for x86 alone, so everything in a dock was trusted. `1088` makes that protection real. The tunnel root ports have no ACS, so a dock's devices join the root port's own IOMMU group, whose domain was picked at boot (passthrough, with `IOMMU_DEFAULT_PASSTHROUGH`), and a device joining an existing group is never checked for trust: docks ran with an identity mapping. External-facing ports now get the translated domain that untrusted devices get, and an untrusted device that would join an identity-mapped group is refused. `1089` has `thunderbolt_platform` report DMA protection when every root port that names the host interface is external facing and behind the SMMU. The PCI glue also asks the IOMMU for pre-boot protection, which no Arm SMMU reports; the N1x firmware has no connection manager and the OS powers the host routers, so no PCIe tunnel exists before the driver creates one.
- `1105` keeps a tunnel root port in D0 while a PCIe tunnel runs through it. The ports cannot wake for a hotplug: they have no `_PRW`, and pciehp turns a port's hotplug interrupt off when it runtime suspends. Their `_S0W` allows D3cold, so with nothing plugged in they suspended, and a dock plugged in later got its display and USB tunnels but never its Ethernet. Omarchy kept all three ports in D0 on the ProArt P14 with a udev rule (`71-omarchy-n1x-usb4-root-ports.rules`); with `1105` they still suspend when nothing is plugged in.

`1083` (2542613815) and `1084` (a2463e2394) are UCSI core fixes from linux-next, which `ucsi_acpi` uses for the USB-C ports. `1083` retries `ucsi_init()` when the EC rejects an early command or reports no connectors, as it already did for a missing role switch; before, one bad answer while the EC was still starting left `/sys/class/typec` empty for the whole boot. `1084` retries re-enabling UCSI notifications on resume, up to five times 500 ms apart; an EC that was still busy when the system woke otherwise left the ports deaf to plug events until reboot. On 7.2.8, which has the upstream workqueue rename (`system_dfl_long_wq`) and `ucsi_debugfs_unregister()`, both apply as they are in linux-next.

Patches 1090–1095 are NVIDIA's SAUCE from `Ubuntu-nvidia-7.0-7.0.0-1022.23_24.04.1`, which has the MT8901 controllers report their sleep states to the SSPM through power_wrap. On this platform the ACPI power resources are empty stubs, so a device only powers down in suspend once the SSPM is told it reached D3:

- `1090` (a255ee68ee85) makes `sspm_ci` tell a request that was never sent (`-EBUSY`) from one whose completion is unknown (`-ETIMEDOUT`).
- `1091` (fff35e933484) gates a whole PCIe host through power_wrap once every root port on it is in D3cold and none is set to wake the system.
- `1092` (053c2c7902c3) adds `xhci-mtk-v2` (`CONFIG_USB_XHCI_MTK_V2`) for the NVDA8000/NVDA8001 controllers, which reports their D3 and D0 in system suspend. It is rebased onto 7.2's `xhci_dbc_remove()`, which takes `enable_mutex`. `1093` (522542575927) adds the 2 ms delay before CRS that these controllers need on resume.
- `1094` (a6a609d1b7ff) and `1095` (03b41c9a041a) bind SPI over ACPI and have the SPI and I2C controllers report D3 in suspend.

`1096` (0f0aeba49253, NVIDIA PR #635, merged after 1022.23) clears the MT8901 EINT event mask for every interrupt armed as a wake source, not only the ACPI event line, so the SPM sees keyboard and touchpad wakes. Without it the platform's deepest sleep state could only be left by the ACPI event line, and a key press did not wake it.

`1097` keeps the generic ACPI xHCI path when firmware marks both supported SSPM mailbox devices absent. The DGX Spark uses the same NVDA8000/NVDA8001 controller IDs as N1x laptops, but can hide that mailbox; requiring Power Wrap there prevents all USB controllers from starting. A present mailbox still requires successful Power Wrap initialization.

NVIDIA's companion watchdog rework (280db0e8cf30) is not carried; it replaces the sbsa_gwdt sleep patch already in `1020` and needs the upstream `early_enable` parameter (1376a013a1, linux-next) first.

The USB4 driver binds only when power_wrap keeps the USB4 resources. `1112` makes that the default on the N1x laptops, which it tells apart from the GB10 desktops (same platform id) by their MediaTek I2C controllers (`NVDA0200`); `power_wrap_drv.usb4_release=` still overrides it. Omarchy still passes the PCI hotplug padding the tunnel root ports need.

`1098` checks for a firmware-present SSPM mailbox before arming the USB4 resource-release hook or probing the SSPM-only ACPI SPI and DSP power drivers. GB10 firmware can describe those devices with SSPM absent. The SPI device-tree path and Power Wrap's other setup are unchanged; a present mailbox that fails still reports its existing errors. This does not enable SPI, audio DSP or USB4 power management on a machine without that interface.

`1111` is NVIDIA's lid-wake fix from NV-Kernels PR #656 (kbutala-nv, LP 2171427), not merged there yet. power_wrap now sends the LPS0 `_DSM` notifications around suspend (3, 7 and 5 in `suspend_noirq`, 6, 9, 8 and 4 in `resume_noirq`), which the EC needs to arm lid wake; Linux only sends them from the x86 s2idle path. Without it, opening the lid did not wake the laptop. In testing on 7.2.8 the first suspend after a lid close woke on its own about 4 s in, on the EC's wake line; logind suspended again 30 s later, and that sleep held for 3 h and woke on lid open.

`1113` makes suspend-to-idle the default on MT8901. The firmware advertises PSCI `SYSTEM_SUSPEND`, so the kernel defaulted to "deep", but the call returns at once and the laptop wakes straight back up. As x86 does for LPS0 systems, it switches when the FADT sets the low-power S0 idle flag, unless `mem_sleep_default=` was given. Omarchy used to pass `mem_sleep_default=s2idle`.

SAUCE that was left out:

- `serial: 8250_mtk: Add ACPI support`, the MT7925 CSA patch and the four cpufreq QoS patches are superseded by 7.2
- the ACPI `_LPI` hierarchical idle series (SAUCE 0039–0054 and 0067–0069) is deferred to the 7.3 port (see [`UPGRADING-7.3.md`](UPGRADING-7.3.md)). Without it, CPU idle uses the flat LPI states only

Patches 1101–1104 are for the ASUS ProArt P14 (H7407BA) keyboard, 0B05:4B42: a keyboard backlight LED for systems without asus-wmi, host-controlled Fn-lock, turning off the keyboard's OOBE mode, which otherwise keeps fading the backlight in and out, and turning the backlight off for sleep. 7.2.6 brought the upstream hid-asus rework that moves the backlight and Fn-lock writes onto one worker (47669bec44fe), so `1101`, `1102` and `1104` were rebased onto it: the LED class device now lives in the driver data, Fn-lock goes through `asus_kbd_fn_lock_set()`, and the use-after-free fix `1101` used to carry is upstream. The upstream Zenbook A16 support (Fn keys) was `1100` until 7.3, which has it (2061075360).

`1109` lets the same 0B05:4B42 keyboard, on the Zenbook A16 and the ProArt P14, send F13-F24. Its key array declares a Logical Maximum of 0x65 under a Usage Maximum of 0xdd, so hid-input dropped every key code above 0x65. The Copilot key sends Left Shift + Left Meta + F23 (report `36 0a 00 72`), lost the F23, and reached userspace as a bare Shift+Meta, so Omarchy's Super+Shift+F23 menu binding never fired. hid-asus already raises that maximum to 0xdd (`QUIRK_FIX_NOTEBOOK_REPORT`), but only when the item sits at bytes 54-55; this keyboard's 144-byte descriptor has it at byte 66. The fixup now finds the key array by its items when the fixed offset doesn't match.

`1107` boots the ProArt P14 and P16 keyboards with media keys on the top row, as the laptops ship; elsewhere `QUIRK_HID_FN_LOCK` keyboards still boot with function keys, and `hid_asus.fnlock_default=` overrides both. `1108` marks their PixArt haptic touchpads (093A:4F02 on the P14, 093A:4F01 on the P16) as pressure pads. They report each contact's force in Tip Pressure without the Button Type feature that would say so, and libinput read the axis as contact size, so light touches (on the P14, every touch) did nothing. Omarchy used to do both from userspace: `hid_asus.fnlock_default=0` in the initramfs and a libinput quirk.

`1106` hides Windows 2022 from `_OSI` on the ProArt P16 H7607BA, the ProArt P14 H7407BA and the Lenovo Pro 9 15N1X11. Their firmware hides `\_SB.SSPM` (NVDA8400) from an OS that claims it, and without the SSPM the I2C controllers behind the keyboard, touchpad and touchscreen never power on, in the installer too. The kernel's per-machine `_OSI` table was only checked on x86; it is now checked from `acpi_osi_init()` elsewhere, unless `acpi_osi=` was given. The Lenovo drops Windows 2022 only, as tested there. The P16 drops every Windows string, as tested there, and so does the P14, whose BIOS 303 started hiding the SSPM like the P16's (302 did not): ASUS's `\MSOS` falls back from Windows 2022 to Windows 2001, which made the EC send the brightness and volume hotkeys only to ASUS WMI.

## Snapdragon laptops

Since `7.2.8-4` this is also the kernel for the Snapdragon X laptops. Their drivers were already on in the config. Four things were missing.

The laptops get no device tree from their firmware, so the package builds the Windows-on-ARM families' trees (`x1*`, `hamoa*`, `glymur*`, `sc8280xp*`) and installs them under `/boot/dtbs/linux-omarchy-n1x/qcom`. They sit under the package's name so that no file is shared with Arch Linux ARM's `linux-aarch64`, which keeps its own in `/boot/dtbs`. Omarchy's Snapdragon setup lists them for the UKI.

`2000` is "media: iris: reset AHB bridge before enabling HW mode" (cc330f558996, in the media tree for 7.4). This config builds the Iris video decoder, and without the fix its firmware can assert when a video starts after the decoder has been idle. It was reported and tested on the Yoga Slim 7x.

`2001` fixes capture from the Snapdragon laptops' digital microphones, which `0511-asoc-updates.patch` broke. It carries mainline's c663014e452d, which builds the codec DMA active channel mask from the port's channel map. `q6dma_hw_params()` never clears that map, and its default mapping covers only one, two and four channels, so a stream gets a mask that does not match its channel count, and the DSP refuses to start the port ("Failed to start APM port 110" on `VA_CODEC_DMA_TX_0`). `2001` clears the map before filling it and maps any channel the default mapping leaves out. Mainline has no fix yet. Tested on the Yoga Slim 7x, through PipeWire and with direct captures of two, four and two channels in a row.

`2002` is "power: supply: qcom_battmgr: Notify charger supplies of online changes" (87fd20da8ca8, [sent on 13 October 2026](https://lore.kernel.org/all/20261013-upstream-battmgr-charger-online-v1-1-731973e133a0@gmail.com/) and applied to the power-supply tree on 17 October). On the X1 and X2, `qcom_battmgr` takes the AC, USB and wireless supplies' online state from the battery's status response, and the firmware sends no notification for them when the charger is plugged in or out, so nothing told userspace. UPower kept the state it read at startup: a laptop booted on the charger never went on battery, and Omarchy stayed on its AC power profile. The patch announces a supply whose online state changes.

`2003` is "wifi: ath12k: don't count a vdev restart as a started vdev" ([sent on 20 October 2026](https://lore.kernel.org/all/20261020213505.31624-1-birk.skyum@pm.me/)). ath12k counted a restart of a running interface as one more started interface, and an access point that changes the channel or bandwidth of a link restarts it. From then on every station statistics request waited 3 s for firmware replies that never came, with the wiphy lock held, until the driver was reloaded. The connection stalled for minutes at a time and group rekeys ended in a disconnect. It hits the Snapdragon laptops with Qualcomm's WCN7850 Wi-Fi whenever the access point changes width on its own, as routers with 160 MHz channels do. Tested on the Yoga Slim 7x through a channel switch and a width change that the access point made during a connection, with no timeout after either.

`2004` to `2006` are Vishnu Reddy's fixes for the Iris video decoder, posted to linux-media and not applied yet. `2004` ([posted on 17 October 2026](https://lore.kernel.org/all/20261017-fix-oob-tss-v1-1-fb86f4cabf65@oss.qualcomm.com/)) stops a read past the end of the timestamp metadata array when a decoded frame matches no stored timestamp, as at the end of a stream. `2005` and `2006` ([posted on 7 October 2026](https://lore.kernel.org/all/20261007-fix-disable-irq-safely-v1-0-45746f9a92de@oss.qualcomm.com/)) fix a deadlock between powering the decoder off and its interrupt thread, which waited for the lock the power-off path held. Unloading `qcom_iris` hung on it, and the runtime suspend after a video stops takes the same path. `2006` is backported to 7.2.8, and the posted version differs only in context. Tested on the Yoga Slim 7x with H.264 clips of 30 to 128 frames, which decoded to the same frames as software decoding and with no UBSAN report (the shipped driver reports one on a 64-frame clip), 20 runtime suspends, and 10 unloads and reloads of the module.

`2210` disables the crypto engine node of the X1 (`hamoa.dtsi`, which the X1P's `purwa.dtsi` includes) and the X2 (`glymur.dtsi`). 7.3 marks the QCE driver broken (df373d39c6f0, "crypto: qce - Mark QCE as BROKEN"), so nothing binds to the node, and fw_devlink keeps the interconnects it names from reaching `sync_state()`. On the X1 those are `aggre2_noc` and the memory controller's `mc_virt`, which then keeps its boot-time vote on DDR, so in suspend the SoC never collapses CX or puts DDR in self-refresh. On the Yoga Slim 7x a suspend on 7.3 without it reached none of those states in 8 minutes. With it both interconnects sync at boot, and a suspend reached all three.

`ARM_SCMI_CPUFREQ` and `ARM_SCMI_PERF_DOMAIN` are built in since `7.2.8-5`. The X1 and X2 CPUs scale through SCMI's performance protocol, and neither module carries an alias, so as modules nothing loaded them: the laptops booted with no cpufreq policies and ran at whatever clock the firmware left. Arch Linux ARM's kernel builds both in. The N1x scales through ACPI CPPC and is unaffected.

Two differences from Arch Linux ARM's kernel matter on these laptops. Omarchy's Snapdragon setup handles both, so the config stays as the N1x needs it.

- The X1 pin control, interconnect, PMIC and bus drivers are modules here and built in there. mkinitcpio's hooks only pick storage, input and display drivers, so the setup names them for the initramfs.
- `IOMMU_DEFAULT_PASSTHROUGH` is on here. Under it the Yoga Slim 7x's keyboard, touchpad and touchscreen read back empty HID descriptors and never appear, so the setup boots with `iommu.passthrough=0`.

- `2110`-`2120` are Microsoft's HID over SPI series, for the touchscreen and pen.
- `2121`-`2156` are the Surface Pro 11's own patches: the platform support and Denali device tree, the Surface Aggregator keyboard cover (tablet-mode resync, LE out-of-band pairing), CAMSS C-PHY, the OV13858 and VD55G0 sensors, and the fixes listed below. `2155` and `2156` are follow-ups to other authors' patches, `2148` and `2145`, kept separate so those stay as their authors wrote them.

The series' backports of mainline 7.3 commits, the geni UART force suspend (`2175`) and root-port D3 on Qualcomm PCIe controllers (`2177`), are in 7.3 itself.

Most of them touch only Surface or Denali code. These also run on other machines:

- Every X1 laptop: the ADSP Modern Standby message (`2176`, `2179`), Jens Glathe's cluster_cl5 drop (`2151`, which stops the DC ZVA hang-then-reset), the QCE sleep vote (`2139`), qcom_battmgr keeping charge limits (`2133`), msm DisplayPort AUX/EDID (`2135`, `2136`), DPU colour blocks after a modeset and the core clock on resume (`2142`, `2145`, `2156`), DP PSR (`2146`), smp2p (`2138`) and CAMSS stream unwinding (`2123`).
- Every Qualcomm SoC on this kernel: the GMU driver for Adreno GPUs that have one (`2170`-`2174`, `2178`).
- Every machine on this kernel, N1x included: Bluetooth LE legacy out-of-band pairing (`2141`, used only when out-of-band data is loaded for a peer) and the arm64 EFI reset skip when restarting from a panic in interrupt context (`2144`).
- Shared code in `2152`: the SPI core can take a pre-mapped coherent receive buffer, spi-geni-qcom gains a QSPI mode that only a device tree asking for it turns on, qcom-geni-se gains the matching serial engine setup, and `platform_profile` works without ACPI, which only matters to a device-tree platform-profile driver like the Surface Pro 11's. The GPI DMA completion workaround in the same patch runs only on `microsoft,denali`.
- Gated to `microsoft,denali`: hci_qca's baudrate event drop for the WCN6750, WCN6855 and WCN7850 (`2148`, limited by `2155`, since it is confirmed only on the Surface Pro 11's WCN7850) and incoming eSCO with Enhanced Accept on the HCI data path (`2149`). Other machines keep upstream's behaviour.

- `2438` makes v3.1.0 SoundWire controllers, which only Glymur's two WSA buses use, reset and re-enumerate on runtime resume instead of exiting clock stop, which on the Zenbook A16 left one bus's amplifiers unattached or returning garbage about two times in three.

### Yoga Slim 7x

`2200`-`2203` and `2207`-`2209` turn on the Lenovo Yoga Slim 7x's webcam, an OmniVision OV02C10 on CSIPHY4 behind the second CCI bus. All but `2207`-`2209` describe only the Yoga's board. They need 7.3's camera clock controller node and the camera nodes from `2161`, and apply after them. The config already builds the CAMSS, CCI, camera clock and OV02C10 drivers.

- `2200`-`2202` are Bryan O'Donoghue's Yoga patches from v7 of "arm64: dts: qcom: Add x1e/Hamoa camera DTSI" (17 September 2026, linked in each patch). They add the camera PMIC's regulators, the `l7b_2p8` supply and the sensor. v7 connects the sensor through a separate `csiphy4` node, whose CSI2 PHY driver 7.2.8 does not have, so `2202` connects it to port 3 of the CAMSS node from `2161` instead, the way the Surface Pro 11's cameras are connected.
- `2203` is Aleksandrs Vinarskis's privacy LED patch from Ubuntu bug 2138706, rebased onto `2202`. The V4L2 core drives the white LED next to the camera (gpio110), so it is on whenever the sensor streams.
- `2207` lists the display's `MDSS_GDSC` as a third power domain of the camera clock controller in `hamoa.dtsi`, so it applies to every X1 laptop's camera, the Surface Pro 11's included. CAMSS delivers no frames once the display subsystem has been powered down for a while, as it is after Omarchy locks the screen and turns the panel off. Holding `MDSS_GDSC` on brings the frames back with the panel off; raising MXC, MMCX or MX, or holding `GCC_DISP_HF_AXI_CLK` or the eDP PHY's supplies, does not. The camera clock controller's binding allows two power domains, so this is an Omarchy patch until upstream settles how to describe the dependency.
- `2208` and `2209` keep the CCI controller from stalling after a sensor on its bus powers down and up again, so they apply to every X1 laptop's cameras. A powered-down OV02C10 holds SCL and SDA low, and a controller that stays clocked from then until its next transfer no longer starts transfers. The first register write after the sensor comes back then times out ("master 1 queue 0 timeout") until the error path resets the controller. Upstream keeps the controller clocked for 1 s after its last transfer and resets it only after a timeout, so a camera started again within about a second failed. `2208` resets the controller every time it resumes, and `2209` suspends it 50 ms after its last transfer. Before these, three patches gave the Yoga's camera rails a 2.5 s minimum off time, which only hid the stall by giving the controller time to suspend first. The Surface Pro 11's `2134` retries the OV13858's first write after the same timeout.

## Config

`config.aarch64` started from NVIDIA's `arm64-nvidia` annotations for the 1021.21 tree, went through `olddefconfig` on 7.2.5, and then had `linux-omarchy`'s behavioral choices applied (preemption, LSM list, zswap and zram defaults, THP, built-in Btrfs, schedutil, I/O schedulers, binder off, no Canonical trusted keys). It sets `linux-omarchy`'s BORE scheduler options too: `SCHED_BORE=y`, `MIN_BASE_SLICE_NS=2000000` and `MQ_IOSCHED_ADIOS=m`.

On 7.2.8 it went through `olddefconfig` again and took the architecture-independent changes `linux-omarchy` made since 7.2.5-6: `RESET_ATTACK_MITIGATION` and `SWIOTLB_DYNAMIC` off, `IMA_WRITE_POLICY` and `IMA_READ_POLICY` on. New symbols kept their defaults (`ACPI_BATTERY_HOOKS=y`; the new hwmon, haptics and camera drivers stay off). `ARCH_MMAP_RND_BITS` stays at the arm64 maximum of 33: `linux-omarchy` moved x86's value to that architecture's default, which says nothing about arm64.

On 7.3 it went through `olddefconfig` once more, with four modules set by hand. `QCOM_SPMI_ADC5_GEN3` is new to the config because 7.3's `hamoa-pmics.dtsi` wires the X1 PMICs' temperature alarms to the PMK8550's ADC5 Gen3, and without it they stay in deferred probe. `HW_RANDOM_QCOM` is 7.3's name for `CRYPTO_DEV_QCOM_RNG`, which `olddefconfig` would otherwise turn off. `AMD_SBTSI` keeps `SENSORS_SBTSI`, which now depends on it. 7.3 marks the Qualcomm crypto engine (`CRYPTO_DEV_QCE`) as broken on Qualcomm SoCs, so it is gone. New symbols keep their defaults.

`FB_NVIDIA` is off since 7.3-3. `nvidiafb` matches every NVIDIA display controller, the N1x GPU included, and Omarchy had to blacklist it.

The Surface Laptop 8 patches add `USB4_QCOM`, built as the `thunderbolt_qcom` module, and `QCOM_SCMI_GENERIC_EXT`, `DEVFREQ_GOV_REMOTE` and `SCMI_QCOM_MEMLAT_DEVFREQ`, built in as on that laptop's own package, and the config builds the Glymur GPU clock controller (`CLK_GLYMUR_GPUCC`), which the X2's GPU needs, and its video clock controller (`CLK_GLYMUR_VIDEOCC`), which `glymur.dtsi` describes, as modules.

The Zenbook A16 patches add its embedded controller driver, built as a module (`EC_ASUS_GLYMUR=m`).

`CONFIG_KEXEC_HANDOVER_ENABLE_DEFAULT` is off since `15`, as in NVIDIA's 63bbd5f0d (LP 2168816). With it on, kexec handover set aside its scratch area as CMA on every boot, about 2.1 GiB on the ProArt P14, and long-term page pins (CUDA host-pinned memory, RDMA registrations) could fail with ENOMEM under memory pressure because pinned pages cannot stay in CMA. Omarchy does not use kexec handover; `kho=on` still turns it on.

## Building

CI builds it on its pull request, on Namespace's aarch64 runners. `skip_build` keeps the large, hardware-specific package out of an unscoped local `bin/build`; to build it locally, name it, natively on aarch64 where possible:

```bash
bin/build --arch aarch64 --package linux-omarchy-aarch64
```

The build produces `Image`, modules and the Snapdragon laptops' device trees; N1x boots through ACPI and needs none. It installs the raw arm64 `Image` as `vmlinuz` for Limine's aarch64 Linux protocol.

## Validation

7.2.8-1 was built natively on the ProArt P14 (2026-10-09), and the NVIDIA open modules 615.78.08 (with our patches) and `acpi_call` compile against it.

7.2.8-3 on the ProArt P14 with `nvidia-open-dkms` 615.78.08 and `omarchy-power-profiles-daemon` (2026-10-10): boot, display, speakers, Wi-Fi, keyboard and touchpad; no energy model registered and the SoundWire link enumerating with its usual handful of attach interrupts. App launches (median of 5, against 7.2.5-10 under the same conditions on AC) were 5-22% faster in the balanced profile (Nautilus 672 to 523 ms, Obsidian 1138 to 958 ms, a terminal 62 to 49 ms) and unchanged in performance. Two suspend-to-idle cycles, 25 minutes (woken by the keyboard) and 7 minutes (lid): CLOCK_MONOTONIC stayed frozen, no watchdog restarts, no SoundWire storm, and audio, Wi-Fi and the compositor back within about 5 s. The ACPI TAD (`rtc1`, the only RTC with a wake alarm) never wakes the system from suspend-to-idle, with the AC and DC timers both armed, so `rtcwake` cannot drive suspend tests on this machine.

`2002` on a Microsoft Surface Pro 11 (X1E80100, 2026-10-13), built as a module against 7.2.8-7 and loaded in place of the stock one, with Omarchy's charger udev rule turned off: plugging in and unplugging the charger now send change events for `qcom-battmgr-ac`, and after restarting UPower on the charger, unplugging sets `OnBattery` within seconds. Without the patch it stayed on AC.

7.2.8-1 with the device trees added, built in an Arch Linux ARM container, on a Lenovo Yoga Slim 7x (2026-10-11), installed beside `linux-aarch64` with the drivers above in the initramfs and `iommu.passthrough=0`. LUKS unlock at Plymouth with the built-in keyboard, the panel at 2944x1840 with the Adreno firmware, touchpad and touchscreen, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, both DSPs, the sound card and the battery reading all work, with no failed units. That boot's UKI carried Arch Linux ARM's device trees. Without `iommu.passthrough=0` the same kernel reached the password prompt with no keyboard. An installer image on this kernel, with the package's own device trees in its live UKI, installs unattended in a VM. `2000` is build-tested on this kernel. On the ProArt P14, 7.2.8-4 (2026-10-11) boots through ACPI with no device tree in its UKI and `qcom_iris` not loaded, app launches stay within 8% of 7.2.8-3 either way, and a 68-minute lid suspend resumes as it does on 7.2.8-3.

7.2.5-6.5 on the ASUS ProArt P14 H7407BA with `nvidia-open-dkms` 615.71.09 (2026-10-02): LUKS unlock at Plymouth, internal display and brightness, CUDA, keyboard (Fn keys, backlight, Fn-lock), touchpad, speakers, headphones and microphones, battery and AC, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, webcam, 122 GiB of RAM with `efi_reclaim_reserved=` (the reclaimed ranges kept a written pattern through display, 16 GiB of CUDA work and suspend-to-idle), suspend-to-idle.
